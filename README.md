# Spatial Catch Allocation MSE

This repository contains management strategy evaluation code, analysis scripts, figure- and table-generation code, and associated manuscript outputs for:

> **Can empirical catch allocation strategies based on single-region stock assessments provide adequate advice for managing spatially structured fish populations?**

## Repository status

This repository and the associated manuscript are under development. The repository should remain private until the authors approve the public release of the code, results, figures, and tables.

Please do not distribute unpublished materials without permission from the authors.

## Study overview

Spatially structured fish populations are often assessed using single-region stock assessment models, even when catch limits are implemented at finer spatial scales. In these situations, stock-level catch advice must be allocated among regions and fleets using empirical allocation rules.

This study evaluates whether a single-region stock assessment, when paired with spatially explicit catch allocation, can provide reliable management advice for a spatially structured fish population.

The analysis uses a closed-loop management strategy evaluation conditioned on the U.S. Atlantic coast black sea bass population, *Centropristis striata*. The operating model represents two population components and two spatial regions corresponding to northern and southern components of the stock.

The study evaluates how the performance of empirical catch-allocation strategies depends on:

* population structure;
* movement directionality;
* movement magnitude;
* temporal variation in movement;
* ontogenetic movement;
* declining recruitment in one population component; and
* density-dependent recruitment.

## MSE framework

The analysis was implemented using the Spatial Processes and Stock Assessment Methods MSE package, [SPASAM-MSE](https://lichengxue.github.io/SPASAM.MSE/).

SPASAM-MSE uses the Woods Hole Assessment Model, or WHAM, as the underlying age-structured, state-space model for both the operating model and estimation model.

The closed-loop MSE contains four linked components:

1. A spatial operating model simulates population dynamics, regional fisheries, regional survey indices, observation processes, movement, and recruitment.

2. Region-specific pseudo-data generated by the operating model are aggregated to represent the information available to a conventional single-region assessment.

3. A single-region estimation model is fitted to the aggregated catch, survey-index, age-composition, and biological data. The estimation model does not explicitly represent regions, movement, or population-component identity.

4. The estimation model generates total catch advice, which is allocated among regions and fleets using alternative empirical catch-allocation strategies. The allocated catches are then applied in the operating model, completing the management feedback loop.

## Simulation design

Each simulation contains:

* a historical conditioning period from 1989 through 2023;
* a 50-year management feedback period from 2024 through 2073;
* biennial stock assessments and two-year management cycles;
* 25 assessment cycles during the feedback period; and
* 100 retained converged realizations for each operating-model scenario and catch-allocation strategy.

The same sequence of random-number seeds was used across allocation strategies within each operating-model scenario to support balanced comparisons.

Total catch advice was generated using a harvest control rule with target fishing mortality set at 75% of the estimated F40% reference point.

## Catch-allocation strategies

Five catch-allocation strategies were evaluated. For strategies that used empirical catch or survey information, allocation proportions were calculated using three-year mean values to smooth annual variation.

### Equal

Total catch advice is divided equally among the four region–fleet combinations.

### Gear-Equal

Total catch advice is first divided between commercial and recreational fleets according to historical fleet catch shares. Each fleet allocation is then divided equally between regions.

### Fleet-Catch

Catch is allocated according to the historical catch share of each region–fleet combination, preserving regional and fleet-specific fishing patterns.

### Index-Equal

Catch is allocated among regions according to regional survey-index values. Each regional allocation is then divided equally between the commercial and recreational fleets.

### Index-Gear

Catch is allocated among regions according to regional survey-index values. Within each region, catch is then divided between fleets according to historical fleet catch shares.

In this study, “gear” refers to the commercial and recreational fleets represented in the operating model.

## Operating-model scenarios

A total of 16 operating-model scenarios were evaluated.

Scenario abbreviations describe population structure, movement, and recruitment assumptions:

* `N`: natal-homing population structure
* `M`: metapopulation structure
* `B`: bidirectional movement
* `U`: unidirectional movement
* `L`: low movement magnitude
* `H`: high movement magnitude
* `I`: IID variation in movement among years
* `O`: ontogenetic movement
* `D`: declining mean recruitment in population component 1
* `BH`: Beverton–Holt density-dependent recruitment

### Main-text scenarios

The six scenarios emphasized in the main manuscript are:

* `NBL`: natal homing, low bidirectional movement
* `MBH`: metapopulation structure, high bidirectional movement
* `MUL`: metapopulation structure, low unidirectional movement
* `MUH`: metapopulation structure, high unidirectional movement
* `NBLD`: natal homing, low bidirectional movement, and declining recruitment
* `NULD`: natal homing, low unidirectional movement, and declining recruitment

### Additional scenarios

The remaining operating-model scenarios are:

* `NBH`
* `MBL`
* `NBLI`
* `MBLI`
* `NBLO`
* `MBLO`
* `NUL`
* `NUH`
* `NBH-BH`
* `MBH-BH`

The full scenario set evaluates alternative combinations of population structure, movement directionality and magnitude, temporal and age-dependent movement, and recruitment dynamics.

## Performance metrics

Management performance was evaluated at both regional and system levels.

The performance metrics include:

* catch;
* spawning stock biomass;
* average annual variation in catch;
* average annual variation in spawning stock biomass;
* probability of overfishing; and
* probability of being overfished.

Short-term and long-term catch and spawning stock biomass were summarized over the first and final 10 years of the feedback period, respectively.

Variation and risk metrics were summarized across the full 50-year feedback period.

The study also uses normalized performance scores. Each metric was scaled from 0 to 1 within each operating-model scenario, spatial scale, and performance metric. Scores were oriented so that higher values represented more desirable outcomes and were averaged across metrics as a descriptive summary of multi-metric performance.

## Main findings

Catch-allocation strategy generally had less influence on system-level outcomes than on regional outcomes. Allocation directly redistributed catch, spawning stock biomass, variability, and depletion risk between regions, even when aggregate performance remained relatively similar among strategies.

Differences among allocation strategies were generally modest under bidirectional movement when recruitment varied around regional means. Stronger regional tradeoffs emerged under unidirectional movement or when mean recruitment declined in one population component.

Equal and Gear-Equal generally produced relatively stable catch outcomes. Catch- and survey-informed strategies were more responsive to persistent regional differences but could increase interannual catch variability.

No single catch-allocation strategy consistently performed best across all operating-model scenarios, spatial scales, and performance metrics.

The results demonstrate that catch allocation is part of the management procedure rather than merely an administrative step following the stock assessment. Allocation strategies should therefore be evaluated at both regional and system levels.

## Reproducing the analysis

The general workflow is:

1. Configure the operating-model scenarios and catch-allocation strategies.
2. Run the closed-loop MSE simulations.
3. Retain the balanced set of converged realizations.
4. Calculate regional and system-level performance metrics.
5. Calculate normalized multi-metric performance scores.
6. Generate the main-text and supplementary figures.
7. Generate the main-text and supplementary tables.

The exact script order, required package versions, computational requirements, and file dependencies will be documented as the repository is finalized.

Large intermediate simulation files should be excluded from version control when they are not required to reproduce the summarized results. File availability and instructions for regenerating or obtaining large outputs will be documented before public release.
